## Laws these drafts are built on

1. **Electronic Communications and Transactions Act (Cap. 43:12, Act 14 of 2014, in force 1 April 2016).** The drafts follow Part VI (consumer protection) and Part VIII (service providers):
   - s33: information a supplier must show (contact details, service description, full price, payment security, terms and how to keep them, access to transaction records), and the chance to review, correct and withdraw an order before placing it. If not complied with, the consumer may cancel within 14 days.
   - s34: perform within 30 days, or the consumer may cancel on seven days' notice.
   - s35: seven-day cooling-off, full refund within 30 days. s32(2)(c) excludes services that began with the consumer's consent within the seven days.
   - s38: marketing emails need the sender's identity and a working opt-out.
   - s42(2) and s44: a hosting provider only has the hosting safe harbour if it designates an agent for take-down notices and publishes the agent's contact details on its website. **This must be filled in before launch.**
   - Section numbers were checked against the 2013 Bill text published by the ITU, which matches the arrangement of the enacted Act. The attorney should confirm against the enacted text.
2. **Data Protection Act, 2024.** Data subject rights, lawful processing, 72-hour breach notification by controllers, and the proviso to section 74 that a copy of personal data transferred abroad remains in Botswana.
